## Objective

Replace the opaque replay cursor with offset addressing on the three tools that carry it, exactly as ADR-0011 decides. A document read becomes the original fetch request re-specified with `offset` and `limit`; the plan key is derived server-side from that request and is never echoed by the caller. `web_fetch` addresses characters, `batch_fetch` addresses items, `research_search` addresses non-empty groups. `next_cursor` and `cursor` disappear from the surface in the same change that introduces the new arguments, so no shipped build offers two ways to address one tool.

**Supersession, stated precisely.** M009 is superseded where it describes the cursor: the `e1.` transport string, `RetrievalCursor`, `CursorPosition`, `CursorBudget`, the 512-byte cap, `cursor_args`, `resolve_page`, `encode_next`, `locate_start`, `page_window`, and the `cursor`/`next_cursor`/`cursor_status` schema keys are removed rather than deprecated. What stands from M009 is token-gated bounded progress (`src/mcp/progress.rs`), the capability-gated Tasks path and its synchronous fallback, the rule that a read is bounded rather than widened (restated below for a tokenless surface), and the requirement that a caller ignorant of the mechanism receives today's payload. M009's closure record is history and is not rewritten. M018 is superseded in its page-budget mechanism only: `page_index`, `page_count`, `page_budget_chars` and `page_cuts` are replaced by `text_from`/`text_to`/`text_total`/`next_offset`, because a window may begin or end at any scalar boundary and splitting a chunk is normal rather than a marked event. M018's defect statements remain the specification of what must be correct — Defect 1 is discharged by window arithmetic replacing chunk-boundary packing, Defect 2 by `focus_scope: "document"`, and Defect 3 by `aggregate_scope`/`total_groups`/`returned_groups`; those three labelling decisions survive unchanged and are emitted by this milestone. M018's own plan is not amended, and its closure record, if one exists, is not rewritten.

## Non-goals

- No new MCP tool, no removed tool, no rename. The inventory stays ten.
- No `cursor` alias, no deprecation window, no compatibility shim reading a legacy token. ADR-0011 rejects coexistence, and the cursor was never released (`Cargo.toml` is at 0.4.4; the cursor appears only under `Unreleased` in `CHANGELOG.md`).
- No byte offsets, no line offsets, no `resources/read` URI, no signed download pointer, no out-of-band document handle.
- No change to `chunk_id` derivation or to `build_document_chunks`' internal clamp; M019 parameterises the budget it is called with. `chunk_id` remains the citation and evidence address.
- No extraction-budget change and no `[fetch].max_chars*` semantics change; that is M019. No `src/fetch/**` change other than populating the document identity already computed at `src/fetch/client.rs:1119`.
- No new ranking, no new `focus` algorithm, no embedding, no model call. Selection is measured as it exists.
- No server-side addressing state, no page cache, no position stored across calls, no persistence of offsets, windows, or documents.
- No addressing for `repo_fetch` or `repo_search`; both are already span-addressed and a second positional mechanism would be ambiguous.
- No new dependency. `base64` stays a direct dependency because `src/meta/challenge/altcha.rs` uses it after `src/mcp/cursor.rs` stops.
- No change to sanitization tiers, trust framing, redirect policy, cache isolation, RRF ordering, capability-skip semantics, or Mode-Off remote denial.

## Invariants that cannot regress

- **Ten MCP tools.** No tool added, renamed, hidden, or re-described out of existence. `tests/mcp_tool_contract.rs` registry parity stays green.
- **Compatibility gate.** A call that sends neither `offset` nor `limit` produces today's payload. For a document that fits one window that is byte identity outright, because the cursor keys never appeared. For a multi-window document the only permitted delta is the absence of the unreleased `next_cursor` and `cursor_status` keys; every other key, value, and type is unchanged, and a test asserts the addressed-key set is empty and the payload minus those two keys equals the recorded baseline bytes.
- **A read never widens a bound.** The retired token carried `d.timeout_ms`; with no token there is nothing to inherit, so the surviving rule is a ceiling, stated as a decision rather than inherited by accident: the effective timeout is `min(caller timeout_ms, [fetch].timeout_ms)`, a read receives exactly that, and no call may exceed `[fetch].timeout_ms` or `[fetch].max_chars_cap`. A caller cannot obtain a longer read by supplying a larger `timeout_ms` or a larger `limit`.
- **Determinism.** Same request plus same `offset`/`limit` yields identical bytes. No re-ranking, no re-sampling, and no clock-, hash-order-, or random-derived value participates in a window. Page composition is a pure function of the addressable text and the clamped window.
- **Stable identity.** FNV-1a derivation in `src/core/identity.rs` is untouched: `doc_id = doc_<16hex(url, title, kind)>`, `chunk_id = chunk_<16hex(doc_id, chunk_index, heading_path)>`. No random UUID enters any evidence field. The plan key remains a 16-hex FNV-1a value.
- **Offsets cannot read outside the cached document.** `text_from <= text_to <= text_total` always holds, and `text_total` is computed from the text the same call obtained. There is no path that indexes a buffer the call did not build.
- **`chunk_id` remains the citation address.** Reading uses offsets; citing uses chunk ids. Neither replaces the other, and no chunk is removed, renumbered, or re-hashed by this milestone.
- Ordinary files stay under 1,600 lines and 80 KB inside existing ratchet ceilings; no code comments; no new dependency; all untrusted I/O stays behind the existing bounded readers. `src/mcp/tools/web_fetch.rs` is 1,149 lines and must stay under the ceiling as it grows.

## Expected production changes

1. **`src/mcp/cursor.rs` is reduced, not deleted, and renamed to `src/mcp/plan_key.rs`.** Decision: retire the codec, keep the derivation. Deleted: `RetrievalCursor`, `CursorPosition`, `CursorBudget`, `CursorError`, `CURSOR_PREFIX`, `MAX_CURSOR_BYTES`, `CURSOR_SCHEMA_VERSION`, `MAX_CURSOR_ID_CHARS`, `PageWindow`, `page_window`, `locate_start`, `encode_next`, `resolve_page`, `ResolvedPage`, `cursor_args`, and the whole `#[cfg(test)]` module that exercises them. Kept and renamed: `PlanKey` unchanged in behaviour, and `CursorTool` → `PlanTool` (`WebFetch`, `BatchFetch`, `ResearchSearch`) because the identity it participates in is now a request-plan key rather than a cursor field. The module is renamed because a module named `cursor` that no longer handles cursors would mislead every later reader; the 499-line file shrinks to roughly 120 lines. `base64` import goes; `src/meta/challenge/altcha.rs` keeps the crate dependency alive.
2. **`web_fetch` gains `offset` and `limit`.** `WebFetchArgs` gains `pub offset: Option<usize>` and `pub limit: Option<usize>` and loses `pub cursor: Option<String>`. The plan key is derived from the request as it is today minus `max_chars`, which becomes a window default rather than an extraction input once M019 lands: `trimmed_url`, `canonicalize_url(final_url)`, `focus`, `focus_max_chunks`, `focus_max_chars`, `include_links`, `extract_mode`, `render`, `browser_profile`. `offset` and `limit` are deliberately excluded, because the key identifies the document and its ordering, not the position inside it; including them would make the guard vacuous.
3. **Addressable text is defined once, on the pre-sanitization basis.** M019 fixes the construction and this milestone emits that number rather than choosing it; the basis is stated exactly once here. Offsets and `text_total` are measured over every chunk text joined by a single `\n` — the exact construction `windowed_fetch` uses today (`src/mcp/tools/web_fetch.rs:1120`) — **before** `strip_control_chars` and **before** `frame`. The reason is that a response must not carry two different numbers under one name: M019 deliberately mints no `text_total` field; it supplies the two conditions that make the number truthful (its invariants 5 and 6: chunk coverage over the document-budget block set is exhaustive and contiguous, and chunk text is not pre-truncated at the caller's budget). This milestone owns the number: it is the scalar count of the joined chunk text, on the same basis as the existing `document.text_chars_returned`, which is already a pre-strip count (`src/mcp/tools/web_fetch.rs:1121` counts `joined` before stripping at `:1122`), so `web_fetch.text_total` uses one coordinate system rather than introducing a second, sanitized one. It is not the same *value* as `document.text_chars_returned`: that field stays window-scoped and keeps its existing meaning, which this milestone does not change. Cutting on the pre-strip basis stays safe and contiguous because `strip_control_chars` is a per-scalar map (`src/core/sanitize.rs:112-126`, with the per-scalar predicate at `:128-155`: unsafe scalars are either replaced by one space or dropped, independently of their neighbours), so `strip(a ++ b) == strip(a) ++ strip(b)`: the caller walks in pre-strip coordinates via `next_offset`, and the union of consecutive windows reproduces the sanitized whole with no gap and no overlap. A window's returned text may therefore be shorter than `text_to - text_from` when the slice contained dropped scalars, and no test may assert those two are equal. The consequence to state plainly: sanitization is an output transform applied to the slice, never a coordinate change.
4. **`windowed_fetch` becomes window arithmetic.** `resolved.window`, `resolve_page` and `encode_next` are replaced by `from`/`to` computation over `text_total`; the page-chunk `Vec<DocumentChunk>` copying stays, restricted to the chunks the window touches, and `document.text_chars_returned`/`text_truncated` keep today's meaning. `with_cursor_keys` (`src/mcp/tools/common.rs:129`) becomes `with_addressing_keys`, which takes an optional window triple plus `next_offset` and declares the key strings once for all three tools.
5. **`batch_fetch` takes index offsets.** `BatchFetchArgs` gains `offset`/`limit` and loses `cursor`. `batch_fetch_plan_key` keeps its exact baseline field list — `max_items`, `max_chars_per_item`, `max_total_chars`, `continue_on_error`, and every item's serialized JSON — and never includes `offset`/`limit`; a caller that alters `items` between windows gets a mismatch, which is the guard's whole purpose. `limit` defaults to `max_items` and is clamped by `[fetch].batch_max_items_cap`. The existing `batch_item_count_truncated` warning is emitted unchanged whenever `item_from > 0 || item_to < item_total`, so the unaddressed truncation message stays byte-identical and an addressed window reuses it instead of inventing a second vocabulary.

Group identity for the guard stays the last card's `stable_id` of each non-empty group (`src/mcp/tools/research_search.rs:278-279`); the internal widening to 14 groups stays, because the aggregates must still be computed over the whole canonical order.

## Storage, protocol, and compatibility effects

Arguments are additive, response keys are additive, and two unreleased keys are removed. No `required` set changes, no value changes type, and no wire string is renamed: `plan_mismatch` and `document_unavailable` are new strings, and `cursor_mismatch` stops being emitted because nothing can emit it.

Compatibility matrix:

| Situation | Behaviour |
|---|---|
| No `offset`, no `limit`, document fits one window | Byte-identical to baseline; no addressing key emitted |
| No `offset`, no `limit`, document exceeds one window | First window as today, plus the removal of `next_cursor`/`cursor_status`; no addressing key emitted |
| `offset`/`limit` supplied | Window keys plus `next_offset` when more units remain |
| `offset == text_total` | Empty window, `text_from == text_to == text_total`, `text == ""`, no `next_offset` |
| `offset > text_total` | Identical to `offset == text_total`; clamped, never a validation failure |
| `limit` above the server maximum | Clamped to `max_chars_cap` (text) or the unit maximum (collections), reported through `structured_warnings` |
| `limit == 0` | `invalid_semantic_value` with `repair.field = "limit"`; never a silent empty page |
| Set or arguments changed between windows | `plan_mismatch`, no partial payload |
| Request produces no addressable document | `document_unavailable`, with a `repair` naming the field to change |
| Unchanged document re-read after a cache miss | Same `doc_id`, same offsets, same bytes |

**What each code means, precisely.** `plan_mismatch` answers "the request I am being asked to index is not the request the window was cut from". `document_unavailable` answers "this request shape produces nothing to address" — a non-text representation, `extract_mode: "metadata_only"`, an unfetched or failed page, or a document with no chunks. Cache eviction is deliberately not `document_unavailable`: an evicted entry is re-fetched inside the same bounded call, and a failed re-fetch is `upstream_failed`. This keeps the three conditions distinguishable instead of collapsing them into one generic failure.

**The `doc_id` retry story, stated accurately.** `doc_id` is the existing deterministic `doc_<16hex(url, title, kind)>` value, so it is stable and never random, but it is a document-identity value rather than a content hash: two different bodies at one URL under one title share it. The guarantee that a re-fetch returns the same bytes comes from `raw_content_hash` inside the derived cache key (`src/fetch/cache.rs:565`), which is why an unchanged document resolves to the same entry and the same offsets while a changed document cannot silently continue a window — it presents different text, and the guard or the addressing arithmetic reports it. A caller therefore retries transparently when `doc_id` and the extraction inputs are unchanged, and re-reads from `offset = 0` otherwise.

No server state is introduced or retained. The plan key never leaves the server, so unlike the cursor it is not a transported value at all and its derivation is free to be reordered in future without a compatibility question; only the offset arithmetic and the cached document are inputs to a window. `architecture/mcp.md` § Pagination, progress, and tasks is rewritten rather than appended to, because its cursor paragraphs (`:254-274`) describe a mechanism that will no longer exist.

## Acceptance and stop conditions

- A caller that sends no `offset` and no `limit` receives today's payload: byte-identical for a single-window document, and identical apart from the removed `next_cursor`/`cursor_status` for a multi-window one, proven by a fixture comparison.
- `offset` and `limit` return exactly `[offset, min(offset + limit, text_total))` in scalar units; `offset >= text_total` returns an empty window with `text_from == text_total` and never an error; `limit == 0` is rejected as repairable `invalid_semantic_value`.
- `text_total` is the true addressable total at the internal budget, so a document larger than one window reports more text than any single window returns. If it merely echoes the caller's truncation, the milestone has not met its premise.
- A response carries exactly one `text_total`, as a top-level `web_fetch` key on addressed reads, on the pre-sanitization document basis; it is not duplicated inside `document`, which keeps its existing members plus the serde-skipped `doc_id`. An unaddressed read of a multi-window document learns only *that* more exists (`document.document_truncated`), and obtains the number by supplying `offset`/`limit`: emitting it on the unaddressed path would add a second delta to a payload whose only permitted change is the removal of two unreleased keys.
- A window boundary that falls inside a multi-byte scalar returns text that never splits the scalar, and offsets computed on pre-framing text remain correct with framing enabled. Consecutive windows compose to the sanitized whole with no gap and no overlap.
- `next_offset` is present exactly when more units remain, for all three tools, and walking it visits every unit exactly once.
- `chunk_id` remains the citation address: it is unchanged in form and value, still appears on every chunk, and no evidence identity depends on an offset.
- No advertised schema or source module mentions a cursor token: `cursor`, `next_cursor` and `cursor_status` are gone from `src/mcp/**`, and every doc site that documented them is updated in the same change — the Pagination paragraph in `docs/config.md`, the section in `architecture/mcp.md`, the stable-code lists in `docs/tool-matrix.md`, `docs/codegg-integration.md`, `architecture/codegg-contract.md` and `skills/eggsearch-mcp/SKILL.md`, and `tests/fixtures/tool_surface/README.md`. `CHANGELOG.md` keeps its historical `Unreleased` line, because changelog history is append-only; the removal is recorded by adding to it, never by editing it.
- `plan_mismatch` and `document_unavailable` are distinguishable from each other and from `upstream_failed`, each with a bounded `repair` hint.
- A read never exceeds `[fetch].timeout_ms` or `[fetch].max_chars_cap`, and a larger caller `timeout_ms` or `limit` cannot widen either.
- The first-slice precision record exists with a corpus, a deterministic measurement, and reported numbers.
- **Fail closed, return for a decision, and do not proceed if:** M019 has not landed (work package 0's expected outcome at this baseline); `text_total` cannot be made larger than a single window without changing extraction; byte identity for the unaddressed caller would require keeping a cursor key; a change would need an eleventh tool, a renamed field, a new `required` key, or a changed existing wire string; removing `doc_id`-adjacent identity would alter `chunk_id` values; or the three tools cannot switch in one landing. In those cases the correct outcome is a recorded blocker, not a partial surface with two addressing schemes.
